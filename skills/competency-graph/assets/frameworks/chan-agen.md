# Agentic Genealogy Framework (Chan AGEN 2026.6)

*Reggie Chan, November 2026.* A competency framework for the agentic genealogist: the practitioner who researches, transcribes, translates, verifies and publishes family history with LLM agents doing much of the work under human verification. Its source-tradition tier covers classical Chinese clan genealogies (zupu 族譜). No professional body publishes such a framework. BCG, ICAPGen, AGRA and the Register of Qualified Genealogists define genealogical competence without agents. The Coalition for Responsible AI in Genealogy (CRAIGEN) sets five principles for AI use but no levels or skills. Read "How this framework was built" before relying on Tier C, A4.5 or B1.3: they draw on one practitioner's project, and Tier C is provisional.

## Structure

- **Tier A: genealogical research foundation (7 domains, 25 competencies):** what any serious genealogist needs, in any record tradition, organised around the Genealogical Proof Standard: planning, record knowledge and search, sources and citation, reading records and their languages, evidence and proof, ethics and professional practice, data and writing. Agents change how this work is done. They do not change what counts as proof.
- **Tier B: Chinese clan genealogy (zupu) (3 domains, 9 competencies):** the source tradition this framework covers in depth: the zupu as a genre, its editions, generation systems, classical formulae, palaeography, names, translation and reference data. Another tradition (parish registers, Korean jokbo, Jewish records) would replace this tier and leave the others unchanged. All nine Tier B competencies are Optional in the pathway, so a practitioner in another tradition completes it without them. A substitute tier for another tradition would join the Optional list in the same way.
- **Tier C: agentic genealogy (5 domains, 21 competencies), provisional:** the disciplines specific to AI tools and agent pipelines. Each builds on Tier A: AI research assistants on evidence analysis, machine transcription on palaeography, gold standards and review on evidence analysis, claim models on data modelling, AI disclosure and privacy-safe release on ethics and publication. Tier A states each standard (cite, separate, redact, convert). Tier C covers only its automation.
- **One pathway, Agentic genealogist,** puts every competency in exactly one of three groups: **Mandatory** (5, the list shared by every pathway in the agentic field, at fixed levels), **Core** (23, the baseline of this pathway, all at L2) and **Optional** (27, of which a person takes six: three to L3 and three to L2). The Optional competencies are listed in four sub-groups, one for each specialisation: Research and specialised evidence, Chinese clan genealogy (zupu), Agentic pipeline engineering, and Publication and family history writing. See "The pathway".

Levels use the competency graph's scale: **L1 Aware** (knows it, has not delivered it), **L2 Practitioner** (delivers it independently), **L3 Advanced** (handles complex cases, is accountable for the result, guides others), **L4 Expert** (a recognised authority beyond their own team or project). **Every L4 needs evidence from outside the person's own work:** a credential, an edited or peer-reviewed publication, adoption by a body or project the person does not run, teaching, or appointment as a reviewer, judge or examiner. Teaching means faculty or juried instruction (e.g. IGHR, SLIG, GRIP or a university course); a single talk does not count. The person's own projects show practice at L2 and L3. They do not show standing.

Where a competency has a near neighbour, its row carries a *Scope* line that says what it covers and which competency covers the rest.

One rule runs through Tier C: **machine output is a proposal, not evidence.** A model's transcription, translation or match enters as a derivative source with its producer recorded. It becomes a conclusion only through review against the image and the proof standard. Agreement between models trained alike counts as one witness. A second rule follows from the first: **ruling on a reading needs the ability to read it.** A person rules in person on a contested reading only at L2 or above in that script and language (A3.1 and A3.4, plus B2.2 for Chinese). Below that, they design the review and send the reading to someone qualified.

## Tier A: genealogical research foundation

### A1. Research planning and record discovery

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1.1 Research questions and plans** (research plan, search profile). | Knows a research question should be narrow and that a plan lists the sources to check | Writes a focused question and plan that puts first the sources most likely to answer it; builds a search profile of names, variants, places and known relatives | Plans projects that span several repositories, languages or jurisdictions; revises the plan as findings arrive; pilots the method on a small sample before scaling | Publishes research-planning methods or templates in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **A1.2 Reasonably exhaustive search** (GPS element 1, search logs, negative searches). | Knows the GPS asks for reasonably exhaustive research and that misses are evidence too | Searches the main catalogues, repositories and platforms for a question; logs every search, including misses | Searches in widening circles (family, community and religious bodies, archives, libraries, overseas collections); normalises search keys across spelling variants, scripts or naming systems; states why the search is reasonably exhaustive | Publishes finding aids, search guides or negative-search evidence in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **A1.3 Record access and witness acquisition** (archives, microfilm, digitisation requests, private holdings). *Scope:* where a record is held and how to get a copy; what the record group contains is A1.4. | Knows records sit in archives, libraries, platforms and private hands, each with its own access terms | Obtains copies or images from archives, platforms and family holders; records where each came from and on what terms | Locates and acquires rare, overseas or restricted witnesses (manuscripts, microfilm, private collections); negotiates with custodians; keeps an inventory of witnesses held and their terms | Brokers access to or digitisation of a collection that an institution then opens to other researchers; advises institutions on access |
| **A1.4 Record types and jurisdictions** (civil registration, census, probate, land, church, immigration, military). *Scope:* what each record group contains, who made it and why; getting a copy is A1.3; reading it in period context is A3.3. | Knows the main record groups for a region and what each typically records | Chooses the record groups that can answer a question for a given place and period; knows what each contains, who created it and why, and its usual gaps | Works across several jurisdictions or record systems; uses the law and administrative practice behind a record to judge what it can prove; traces records moved by boundary and jurisdiction changes | Publishes a record guide for a region or record type in an edited venue or for a repository, or teaches a region's records as course faculty |

### A2. Sources, citation and provenance

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A2.1 Source, information and evidence classification** (original vs derivative, primary vs secondary, direct, indirect and negative evidence). | Knows original and derivative sources, primary and secondary information, and direct, indirect and negative evidence | Classifies each source, information item and evidence item before relying on it | Weighs compiled genealogies, transcriptions and machine output by their distance from the event and the informant; treats every transcription as derivative until checked against the image | Publishes evidence-classification methods for new kinds of source in an edited or peer-reviewed venue, or teaches them as course faculty |
| **A2.2 Source citation** (GPS element 2, layered citations). *Scope:* the citation and its standard; carrying citations through an automated pipeline is C4.1. | Knows a citation must let another person find the exact source | Writes complete, consistent citations, including page, image and frame identifiers; puts a citation on every image reproduced | Writes layered citations for derivative and digitised sources (original, film, image, transcription, machine output); cites so that any reader can reach the exact page, image or frame | Sets citation standards that a society, journal or publisher the person does not run adopts, or publishes on citation in an edited venue |
| **A2.3 Provenance and custodial history** (custody chain, transmission, edition lineage). *Scope:* the history of any source; applying it to zupu editions is B1.2. | Knows that who made a record, when, and who has held it since affect its weight | Documents a source's creator, date, custody and reproduction history | Reconstructs the transmission history of copied and re-edited works (which copy descends from which); explains differences between witnesses by that history | Publishes provenance studies in an edited venue, or ones that a library or archive uses in its catalogue |

### A3. Reading and extracting historical records

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A3.1 Palaeography and transcription** (old hands, transcription conventions). *Scope:* any script; Chinese script forms add B2.2. | Knows transcription conventions: verbatim text, marked gaps and uncertain readings, no silent correction | Transcribes records in at least one script accurately, keeping spelling, punctuation, capitals and marks as written; includes headings, insertions and endorsements; notes damage that affects the reading; marks uncertainty, gaps and the transcriber's own comments; never fills a gap from expectation | Reads difficult hands, damaged pages and unusual layouts; settles uncertain characters with named, checkable evidence; checks other people's and machines' transcriptions | Recognised authority on a script or record type: holds a palaeography qualification, teaches palaeography as course faculty, or sets transcription conventions that an archive, society or publication the person does not run adopts |
| **A3.2 Abstracting and structured extraction** (abstracts, entry anatomy, extraction spreadsheets). | Knows the difference between a transcription, an abstract and an extract | Abstracts records into structured fields (person, relationship, date, place) with a pointer back to the source, keeping the meaning, quoting ambiguous wording, leaving out boilerplate and marking any comment as the abstractor's | Designs extraction schemas from a record type's entry anatomy; handles entries that do not fit the schema without forcing them | Designs extraction standards that collections or projects the person does not run adopt |
| **A3.3 Calendars, places and historical context** (calendar conversion, jurisdictions, gazetteers, migration). *Scope:* includes all calendar and era-date conversion; Chinese reference databases are B3.1. | Knows that dates and place names change over time and must be read in period context | Converts dates between calendars, including regnal and era dates; identifies historical places and jurisdictions; uses period context to interpret a record | Handles ambiguous dates, renamed or abolished places and period-specific terms; keeps place and migration notes that others can check | Publishes reference tools (converters, gazetteers, chronologies) in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **A3.4 Record languages and translation** (Latin, older vernaculars, classical languages, formulaic and legal language). *Scope:* reading and translating the language of the records in any tradition; script forms are A3.1; Chinese register and official titles are B2.4; machine translation is C2.4. | Knows the record languages of the regions and periods they research, and when a translation is needed | Reads and translates standard records in at least one record language that is not their own modern language (e.g. Latin parish entries, older legal forms of their own language, classical Chinese) with a glossary | Handles legal, abbreviated and formulaic language; checks other people's and machines' translations | Publishes translations or record-language guides in an edited venue, or teaches record languages as course faculty |

### A4. Evidence analysis and proof

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A4.1 Analysis and correlation of evidence** (GPS element 3). | Knows evidence must be compared across sources, not read one source at a time | Correlates evidence from several sources in tables or timelines to test a hypothesis | Correlates large or conflicting bodies of evidence; recognises when apparent agreement comes from shared dependence on one source | Teaches correlation methods as course faculty, or reviews analyses for a journal, credentialing body or society |
| **A4.2 Identity resolution and kinship determination** (same-name problems, merges and splits, parent-child proof, one person's cluster). *Scope:* one person's identity and parent-child links, including research in that person's cluster of family, friends, associates and neighbours (FAN); studying a defined group is A4.5. | Knows that the same name is not the same person and that each parent-child link needs its own evidence | Separates or merges individuals on evidence; uses associates and neighbours to separate or identify individuals; documents parent-child links across three generations | Resolves hard identity cases (common names, aliases, adoption, name changes), including through structured cluster research; keeps birth and adoptive lineages distinct; avoids false merges and false splits at scale | Publishes identity or kinship solutions in a peer-reviewed journal, or designs identity-resolution methods that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **A4.3 Conflict resolution and proof arguments** (GPS elements 4 and 5, proof summaries). | Knows conflicting evidence must be resolved or explained, not ignored | Writes a proof summary that resolves a conflict and reaches a conclusion on the strongest evidence | Writes proof arguments for indirect- and conflicting-evidence problems; states what would overturn the conclusion; holds a question open when evidence is insufficient | Publishes proof arguments in peer-reviewed journals; reviews others' proofs for a journal or credentialing body |
| **A4.4 Genetic genealogy evidence** (autosomal, Y-DNA and mtDNA tests, shared matches). | Knows the test types (autosomal, Y-DNA, mtDNA) and what each can and cannot show | Uses DNA matches and shared-centimorgan ranges to test a documentary hypothesis; knows Y-DNA can test a documented paternal line | Correlates DNA with documentary evidence in proof arguments, including Y-DNA tests of documented paternal lines; handles endogamy and misattributed parentage; follows informed-consent rules for testers | Publishes DNA case studies in a peer-reviewed venue, or contributes to genetic-genealogy standards that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **A4.5 Prosopography and cluster research** (collective biography, group and network reconstruction). *Scope:* a defined group (a lineage, a cohort of officials or examination graduates, a parish or migrant community) and the ties among its members; using one person's cluster to identify that person is A4.2; CBDB stays in B3.1 as the Chinese reference tool for this work. | Knows what prosopography is and when a question concerns a group rather than one person | Builds a group list from records with each member's source; maps the ties among members (e.g. witnesses, sponsors, neighbours, co-migrants) | Defines a population with explicit inclusion rules, records each member's identification evidence, and draws group conclusions that state their coverage and limits (e.g. officeholders, a parish, a migrant cohort; quantitative tests where the numbers support them) | Publishes prosopographical work in an edited or peer-reviewed venue, or builds a reference population that a body or project the person does not run adopts |

### A5. Ethics, privacy and rights

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A5.1 Privacy of living persons and consent** (living-person data, release tiers). *Scope:* the policy and judgement on what may be shared; enforcing it automatically is C5.2. | Knows living people's data needs protection and that the law differs by jurisdiction | Withholds or redacts living people's details from shared work unless they have consented | Sets release tiers by recency and surface (private, internal, redacted, public); records consent for specific passages; checks for harm to living relatives through inference | Sets privacy policy that a society, platform or archive adopts |
| **A5.2 Copyright, licensing and terms of use** (image rights, platform terms, licences). | Knows images, transcriptions and databases carry rights and terms of use | Checks terms before reusing images or data; licenses own work explicitly | Separates rights layers (third-party sources, own research, code); documents each source's redistribution status; clears permissions for publication | Advises institutions or societies other than their own on rights policy |
| **A5.3 Family collaboration, testimony and cultural respect** (oral history, family informants, community archives). | Knows family testimony is evidence with its own strengths and limits, and that communities have a stake in their records | Records testimony with who said it and when; credits collaborators; keeps testimony distinct from documented fact | Defines the scope of each informant's authority; runs correction and consent processes with family and community bodies (e.g. family associations, clans, congregations); follows a community's own rules for its data before putting records through AI tools; labels tradition and cultural claims as such | Is recognised by a community body, society or archive the person does not run for leading community genealogy work across families or associations, or publishes on community practice in an edited venue |
| **A5.4 Professional practice and client work** (client reports, contracts, fees, codes of conduct). *Scope:* research commissioned by another person; the research craft itself is A1 to A4. | Knows the professional codes (e.g. APG, BCG) and that client work needs an agreed scope, fee and report | Agrees scope, fees and deliverables in writing; delivers a research report a client can act on, which starts from what was already known and reports what was searched and not found; keeps client data confidential | Runs several commissions at once; reports inconclusive results and their limits plainly; handles conflicts of interest, scope changes and disputes | Serves as a judge, examiner or ethics officer for a professional body, or teaches professional practice as course faculty |

### A6. Genealogical data management

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A6.1 Genealogical data modelling** (persons, relationships, unions, events, sources). *Scope:* the data model, including keeping attestations apart from conclusions; recording machine-produced claims and their review state is C4.1. | Knows the core entities: person, relationship, union, event, source and citation | Records data in a genealogy database or spreadsheet with a source linked to each fact | Designs schemas for hard cases (adoption recorded so that both birth and adoptive lineages remain traceable, plural marriage, succession and heirship, variant accounts across sources); keeps source attestations separate from conclusions | Designs data models that projects or tools the person does not run adopt |
| **A6.2 Genealogy software and data exchange** (GEDCOM 7, GEDCOM X, FamilySearch, Gramps). | Knows GEDCOM and the main genealogy programs and platforms | Exports and imports GEDCOM between tools and checks what was lost | Writes and validates exports to an exchange standard (e.g. GEDCOM 7) from any tool or database, mapping non-standard data (extensions, non-Latin names, variants) without silent loss | Contributes accepted changes to exchange standards, or to widely used genealogy tools the person does not run |

### A7. Writing and publication

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A7.1 Genealogical writing and narrative** (proof summaries, compiled genealogies, family histories). | Knows the standard forms: proof summary, research report, compiled genealogy, family history | Writes a research report or family sketch with citations, in a format accepted in the field, with a clear line between fact and inference; places the family in its historical and community setting | Writes book-length family history that keeps uncertainty visible; uses a numbering system; labels each claim by its basis (e.g. documented, inferred, traditional) | Publishes family history in edited or peer-reviewed venues, or holds an award for it from a body the person does not run |
| **A7.2 Book and archive production** (print, web, typesetting, ISBN, legal deposit). *Scope:* what a finished publication must meet; regenerating outputs automatically is C5.4. | Knows the publication surfaces (print, PDF, website, database) and their obligations | Produces a print-ready or web publication from structured sources | Produces book and web editions that meet publication obligations (identifiers such as ISBN, legal deposit, rights notices); typesets non-Latin scripts where needed; checks every surface against the data before release | Sets production standards that a publisher, society or series adopts |
| **A7.3 Corrections, revision and continuation** (errata, new editions, continuation of the lineage). | Knows published genealogy needs a way to correct errors and add new generations | Keeps a correction log; issues errata and dated revisions | Runs a revision protocol: correction intake, re-review, versioned releases and continuation of the lineage in later editions | Designs revision or continuation processes that a society, family association or long-running project the person does not run adopts |

## Tier B: Chinese clan genealogy (zupu)

### B1. Zupu structure and editions

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1.1 Zupu genre and anatomy** (族譜, 家譜, 宗譜; 序, 凡例, 世系圖, 世系錄). | Knows what a zupu is and its usual parts: prefaces, compilation rules, charts, entries, regulations, tombs and property | Reads a zupu cold: identifies its parts and compilation rules and how charts and entries refer to each other | Reads unusual or damaged zupu and their back matter (contracts, honours, testaments, donor rosters); interprets entries by the compiler's own rules; explains the politics behind prefaces | Recognised authority on the zupu genre: publishes on it in an edited or peer-reviewed venue |
| **B1.2 Editions, recompilation and witness comparison** (版本, manuscript vs print, collation 校勘). *Scope:* zupu editions; the general method is A2.3; automated matching of persons is C4.3. | Knows zupu were recompiled and recopied, so one lineage can have several editions | Identifies a zupu's edition and finds other editions in catalogues (Shanghai Library, FamilySearch, university libraries) | Compares editions entry by entry; classifies divergences by cause (copying error, deliberate revision, new information); counts editions as independent witnesses only when their history shows they are | Publishes edition studies or catalogues in an edited venue, or ones that a library adopts |
| **B1.3 Lineage, branch and generation systems** (房, 支, 派, 世; 字輩 generation poems). | Knows generation numbering, branches and houses, and generation poems | Places a person in the right generation and branch; uses generation characters and sibling name sets as checks | Aligns generation counts across branches and editions; links persons across pages and branches from the compiler's own cross-reference conventions (e.g. literary-name references) when generation characters do not settle it | Publishes generation-alignment or disambiguation methods in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts |

### B2. Classical Chinese records

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B2.1 Genealogical formulae and kinship terms** (配, 繼配, 子, 出繼, 嗣, 兼祧, 諱). | Knows the common entry formulae (配, 子 with a count, 字, 號, 葬) | Reads standard entries correctly, including wives, children, succession and burial formulae | Treats adoption and succession (嗣, 過繼, 兼祧) as distinct relationship types; reads honorific and taboo conventions that change meaning; settles formula ambiguity from the compiler's rules | Publishes formula glossaries or reference guides in an edited venue, or ones that a library or research guide the person does not run adopts |
| **B2.2 Chinese palaeography and variant characters** (異體字, cursive forms, taboo forms, vertical text). *Scope:* reading Chinese script forms; ruling in person on a Chinese reading in C3.2 needs L2 here. | Knows vertical right-to-left reading, variant characters and cursive forms | Reads printed and clear manuscript zupu; marks uncertain glyphs instead of guessing | Reads cursive, damaged and variant forms; settles hard characters from their components with the evidence recorded; recognises taboo-avoidance forms and copyist confusions | Recognised palaeographic authority for a period or region: publishes in an edited venue, teaches as course faculty, or is consulted by libraries |
| **B2.3 Chinese names and romanisation** (名, 字, 號, 諡; romanisation systems, dialect forms). | Knows the name types (given, courtesy, literary, posthumous) and that romanisation systems differ | Records each name type in its own field; romanises consistently to a declared standard (e.g. Hanyu Pinyin, ALA-LC, Jyutping); keeps dialect romanisations as their own forms | Resolves names across editions and name types; keeps name-variant lists; labels provisional readings of polyphonic characters; follows a declared rule for rendering personal names (romanise, gloss or translate) | Sets naming and romanisation conventions that a library, catalogue or publisher adopts |
| **B2.4 Translation register and annotation** (documentary vs readable translation, official titles, allusions). *Scope:* Chinese register, official titles and allusions; reading and translating record languages in general is A3.4; running and checking machine translation is C2.4. | Knows classical Chinese is terse and that renderings of titles and kin terms vary | Translates entries in a declared register with a glossary of titles and kin terms; keeps the Chinese beside the English | Keeps literal and readable translations separate and checkable against each other; renders official titles to a cited standard (e.g. Hucker); annotates dates, places, figures and allusions | Publishes translations in an edited venue, or translation standards that a body or publisher the person does not run adopts |

## Tier C: agentic genealogy (provisional)

### C1. Agent use, pipeline design and orchestration

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1.1 Agentic workflow design** (stages, contracts, review points). | Knows an agent pipeline needs defined inputs, outputs, limits and a human review point | Runs a defined agent workflow on a task (e.g. transcribe and translate a page) with a template and a review step | Designs multi-stage pipelines in which each stage's inputs, outputs and limits are explicit, and every stage that can change a conclusion has a human checkpoint and an automated check; sizes work units so each can be reviewed | Designs agentic genealogy methods that projects the person does not run adopt, or publishes them in an edited venue |
| **C1.2 Agent roles, delegation and independence** (producer, reviewer, model choice). | Knows that a reviewer who also did the work is not independent | Scopes each agent task so its output can be reviewed in full; keeps producer and reviewer separate | Matches tasks to models by capability and cost; records which model or person produced and reviewed each output and whether the reviewer was independent; fixes a failing step instead of adding duplicate runs | Sets delegation and independence policy that other projects or organisations adopt |
| **C1.3 Domain skills, templates and tooling** (configuration, prompts, templates, extensions). | Knows the configuration, prompts, templates and extensions of the agent or HTR tools in use (e.g. instruction files, custom models, plugins) | Writes project instructions and templates that encode domain rules (e.g. name pitfalls, authority rules, conventions) | Packages workflows as reusable templates, scripts or extensions, with safeguards that block prohibited operations; keeps them current as rules change; tests changes before relying on them | Publishes genealogy tooling in an edited venue or open repository, and a body or project the person does not run adopts it |
| **C1.4 AI research assistants and search** (chat assistants, AI record hints, full-text search, document summaries). *Scope:* using AI tools in one's own research; building pipelines is C1.1 to C1.3. | Knows chat assistants and AI search tools can find, summarise and misstate records, and that their answers are leads to check | Uses AI search, record hints, full-text search of handwritten records and document summaries to find leads; checks each against the record itself before relying on it; cites the record, not the assistant | Chooses AI tools by tested reliability for a record type, allowing for what a tool was trained on and whom it under-represents; designs prompts and checks that expose invented records and citations; records which tool produced each lead | Teaches responsible AI-assisted research as course faculty, or publishes evaluations of AI research tools in an edited venue |

### C2. Image preparation and machine reading

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C2.1 Image capture and preparation** (resolution, page and frame identifiers, derivatives). | Knows resolution, colour and format affect machine reading | Prepares images at a suitable resolution with stable page and frame identifiers; maps spreads, sides and frames | Builds page maps across editions and media (manuscript, print, microfilm); keeps every derivative image traceable to its original page or frame | Sets imaging and identifier standards that an archive, library or project the person does not run adopts |
| **C2.2 Layout analysis and entry cropping** (segmentation, reading order, entry crops). | Knows layout and reading order are separate problems from character recognition | Crops entries or columns from page images for review | Checks segmentation against the page so no entry is clipped, merged or mixed with a neighbour; keeps each segment traceable to its page coordinates so it can be cited; handles vertical, multi-column and tabular layouts | Publishes segmentation methods or benchmarks for a record type in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **C2.3 Multi-witness machine transcription** (OCR, HTR, vision-language models). *Scope:* producing and checking machine readings; ruling on a contested reading is C3.2. | Knows machine transcription of historical text has high error rates and can invent content | Produces a machine transcription and lists its divergences from at least one independent check (a second engine or a human pass) | Chooses or trains engines by measured accuracy for each script and layout; counts agreement between similarly trained models as one witness; routes readings that would change a claim to a reader qualified in the script | Publishes transcription pipelines, trained models or results in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts as a benchmark |
| **C2.4 Machine translation pipelines** (parallel renderings, glossary control). *Scope:* running and checking machine translation; the translator's own skill is A3.4 (and B2.4 for Chinese register and titles). | Knows machine translation can drop or invent content and flatten terms | Checks machine translations against a glossary and at least one independent check (a second rendering or a human reader) | Builds translation pipelines that record disagreements between renderings, apply glossary and naming rules automatically and link each translation to the transcription version it came from; routes disputed meanings to a qualified translator | Sets translation-pipeline standards that projects or publishers the person does not run adopt |

### C3. Verification and evaluation

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C3.1 Gold standards and benchmarks** (gold pages, character error rate, false-person rate). | Knows a gold standard is a human-verified answer key for testing models | Builds a small human-verified gold set and measures a model's character error rate on it | Benchmarks models on genealogy-specific errors (false persons, false merges and splits, wrong relationships, lost citations), on silently normalised spelling and invented text, and on names, dates and places separately, as well as character error; reruns benchmarks when models change | Publishes benchmarks or metrics in an edited venue, or ones that a body or project the person does not run adopts |
| **C3.2 Independent review and adjudication** (contested readings, adjudication rules, reviewer agreement). *Scope:* deciding whether machine output stands; ruling in person on a reading needs L2 in that script and language (A3.1 and A3.4, plus B2.2 for Chinese). | Knows machine output is a proposal until a person reviews it | Reviews agent output against the source image and records a judgement for each item, within the scripts and languages they can read | Designs independent review with recorded evidence; sends contested readings to reviewers qualified in the script and language and has them decided by declared rules; escalates to the project's editorial authority (editor, family body or client) where needed; measures reviewer agreement | Designs review and adjudication protocols that projects the person does not run adopt |
| **C3.3 Automated validation and gates** (validators, coverage checks, hooks). | Knows deterministic checks catch errors that models and reviewers miss | Runs validators that compare the database with its sources and report differences | Builds validation gates where data is written (schema, integrity, structural constraints such as a stated child count matching the children recorded, coverage), with reviewed exceptions listed; blocks release on failure | Sets validation standards that other genealogical data projects adopt |
| **C3.4 Verification of agent output** | Knows that an agent saying work is done or correct is a claim, not proof, and that models produce fluent errors | Checks agent output against the source, a test or an independent check before relying on it; records what was checked | Builds verification into the work (independent reviewers or tests, checking rates set by risk); measures the error that gets through; guides others | Sets verification standards adopted beyond their own team or project |

### C4. Claims, provenance and data integrity

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C4.1 Claim and provenance modelling** (machine-produced claims, review states, supersession, PROV). *Scope:* how a system records who or what produced each claim and its review state; the citation standard is A2.2; separating attestations from conclusions is A6.1. | Knows a claim should carry its source, its producer (person or model) and its status | Records claims with source pointers and a status (proposed, reviewed, accepted) | Supersedes reviewed claims instead of overwriting them; checks automatically that citations survive every processing stage; records which model, prompt and version produced each output | Designs provenance models that projects or tools the person does not run adopt |
| **C4.2 Guarded data changes** (reviewable, reversible and atomic writes). | Knows unattended agent writes to a genealogy database can destroy work | Makes agent writes reviewable and reversible (e.g. preview and approval, backups, version control) | Makes multi-record changes atomic, reviewable before they apply and verifiable after; detects conflicting concurrent changes | Designs change-control systems that projects the person does not run adopt |

| Competency | L1 Aware | L2 Practitioner | L3 Advanced | L4 Expert |

## How this framework was built

- **Tier A** rests on published standards: the BCG *Genealogy Standards* and the Genealogical Proof Standard, the requirements of the credentialing bodies (BCG, ICAPGen, AGRA, the Register of Qualified Genealogists) and standard methodological texts. Three 2026.2 additions answer the first adversarial review's list of missing competencies: record types (A1.4), client practice (A5.4) and AI research assistants (C1.4). Record languages (A3.4) answers the re-review. **The author added A4.5 Prosopography and cluster research because his own work includes prosopography; neither review asked for it.**
- **Tier B** rests on published zupu scholarship and library guides (see "Mapping to other references"), and on the author's own reading of zupu.
- **Tier C was derived mainly from one practitioner's own agentic zupu project:** the author's multi-edition zupu digitisation and publication work (manuscript, print and microfilm editions, a lineage database, verified pages, a typeset book and a public archive site). A November 2026 review of OCR, provenance and AI-in-genealogy sources checked it. No other project or practitioner has tested it. **Tier C is provisional.** It describes what one working pipeline needed, restated as general capability, and it may miss methods that other projects use well.
- **Review so far.** An adversarial review and a re-review in November 2026 found that several descriptors copied that project's house method and that one pathway let a person rate L3 in judging readings of a script they could not read. Edition 2026.2 restates those descriptors as outcomes, gives every L4 an outside test, fixes the pathway logic and lists the prerequisites it checks. B1.3's L3 was first written from the same project's disambiguation method and has been restated in general terms. An owner whose evidence comes from that project should not read a high match against Tier C, A4.5 or B1.3 as independent validation.
- **What outside teaching covers (2026.5).** The teaching and society guidance found in November 2026 cover the user layer of Tier C: responsible use, prompting, summarising, extracting facts, image analysis, AI tools on genealogy sites, conflict tables, translation and writing help. That supports C1.4, C5.1, C5.2 and the L1 to L2 rows of C2.4 and C5.3. None of it covers agent pipelines, gold sets, claim models or guarded data changes (C1.1 to C1.3, C2.1 to C2.3, C3 and C4), which still rest on one project and the handwriting-recognition literature.
- **Sources and limits.** Three claims once marked as not verified were checked against their sources in November 2026 (see "Changes in 2026.5"). Two limits remain and are stated where they apply: the 中國家譜總目 figure was not checked against the volumes, and Evidence Explained's section on citing AI was not read in the book.

## Outside review wanted

The framework stays a draft until outside practitioners review it. Wanted:

- **Tier A:** a BCG Certified Genealogist or an ICAPGen Accredited Genealogist, to check the descriptors and the credential mapping against current practice.
- **Tier B:** a zupu or Chinese-lineage scholar, or a librarian who catalogues Chinese genealogies, to check the genre, edition, formula, palaeography, naming and translation descriptors.
- **Tier C:** a digital-humanities or HTR practitioner (for example, someone who trains handwritten-text-recognition models for an archive), to check whether the agentic descriptors hold beyond one project.

Reviewers' findings and the edition they reviewed will be listed here.

## Use in the competency graph

This is the level guide for the library framework `chan-agen`; its requirements are in `chan-agen.csv` beside this file, on the L1–L4 scale. Competencies that match carry `externalRef: "Chan AGEN 2026: <competency>"`. Select it under `frameworks:` in `competency-graph.yaml` (`frameworks.py list --all` shows the pathway), and the dashboard's Competency frameworks tab lists it. When rating a competency against it, read that competency's row in the tables above: each column says what the evidence must show at that level.
